# Competitive Landscape: Home Care (Complex & Domiciliary) — Maidstone & Kent, UK

**Client:** Continuity of Care Services (CCS)  
**Current site:** https://www.continuitycareservices.co.uk  
**Scope:** Domiciliary and complex home care providers serving Maidstone and Kent.  
**Date:** February 2025

---

## 1. Market overview

- **Care Choices** lists **176 home care providers** in Maidstone alone, covering complex care, dementia, palliative, and condition-specific support ([Care Choices – Maidstone home care](https://www.carechoices.co.uk/browse/south-east/kent/maidstone/homecare/)).
- Demand is split between **visiting (domiciliary)** and **live-in** care; many providers offer both plus specialist complex care.
- **CQC regulation** is mandatory; ratings (Outstanding / Good / Requires Improvement) are a key trust signal for families.
- **Funding mix:** Private, Local Authority, and NHS (e.g. CHC); several competitors state they accept all three.

## 3. Porter’s Five Forces (summary)

| Force | Intensity (1–5) | Notes |
|-------|------------------|--------|
| **New entrants** | 3 | Registration and compliance (CQC, insurance, recruitment) create barriers; local reputation and staff supply limit speed of entry. |
| **Supplier power** | 3 | Staff (carers) are the main “supplier”; shortage of skilled workers gives them some power; agency vs employed models vary. |
| **Buyer power** | 4 | Many small clients; but families can compare 176+ providers, switch if unhappy, and are price-sensitive; LA/NHS terms also set constraints. |
| **Substitutes** | 3 | Residential care, family-only care, day centres, technology (telecare). Switching cost and emotional attachment to “home” reduce substitution. |
| **Rivalry** | 4 | Many local players; similar core services (domiciliary, complex, respite); differentiation via CQC rating, consistency of carers, and brand. |

**Overall:** Moderate attractiveness; **differentiation and trust** (CQC, “same carer”, clear positioning) matter more than price alone.

## 4. Positioning and differentiation

### 4.1 Consistency of carers (“same carer / no rotation”)

Several Maidstone/Kent providers already emphasise **consistent teams**:

- **Home Instead:** Relationship-led matching; care professionals “part of the family”; no uniforms; 1-hour minimum visits ([Home Instead Maidstone](https://www.homeinstead.co.uk/maidstone/local-home-care-services/home-care/)).
- **Angels Home Care:** “Smaller, close-knit team”; “same friendly faces at every visit”; 15-mile radius; continuity over rotation ([Why Us – Angels Home Care](https://angelshomecarekent.co.uk/why-us/)).
- **CCS (live site):** “We don’t rush or rotate staff every other week”; “get to know each person, not just their care plan”; “Your Team, Your Time, Your Life” ([Continuity of Care Services](https://www.continuitycareservices.co.uk)).
- **Absolute Home Care:** Carer choice at assessment; “trust and confidence” in the person ([Absolute Home Care FAQs](https://absolutehomecarekent.co.uk/faqs/)).

**Implication:** “Same carer / consistent team” is a **shared** differentiator, not unique. CCS should sharpen **how** it delivers this (e.g. how matching works, how many carers per client, guarantees) and pair it with **proof** (testimonials, CQC “caring”/“responsive”, retention stats).

### 4.2 Positioning map (conceptual)

```
High "relationship / consistency" focus
    |
    |  ★ CCS          ★ Angels Home Care    ★ Home Instead
    |
    |  ★ Helping Hands (complex/dementia)   ★ Bluebird
    |
Low |__________________________________________________________ High
    Generalist / volume              Specialist / complex
```

- **CCS** sits with Angels and Home Instead on “relationship / consistency”; against **Helping Hands** and others on “specialist / complex” (epilepsy, PEG, mobility, 24/7).
- **Opportunity:** Own “**consistent team + complex care**” in one offer (same carer even for complex/clinical), which many nationals or volume-focused agencies do not promise.

### 4.3 CQC and trust

- **Home Instead Maidstone** markets its **Outstanding** CQC rating strongly.
- **CCS** is **Good**; the live site states “Proud to be rated ‘Good’ by the CQC.”
- **Maidstone Home Care Limited** has **Requires Improvement** in Safe, Caring, Well-led — a weakness vs CCS and Home Instead.

**Implication:** Lead with “Rated Good by CQC” and, where relevant, “consistent team” and “complex care at home” so the site clearly communicates both **trust** and **capability**.

## 6. Strategic implications for CCS

1. **Differentiation:** Emphasise **consistent carers + complex care** in one offer (domiciliary, respite, complex, palliative, 24/7), with clear proof (CQC Good, testimonials, “Your Team, Your Time, Your Life”).
2. **Messaging:** Keep “no rotation / same team” but add **concrete** detail (e.g. small team, matching process, typical number of carers per client) so the claim is distinguishable from Angels and Home Instead.
3. **Trust:** Prominently show CQC Good and, if available, “Caring” and “Responsive” domain ratings.
4. **Audience:** Live site supports **children and adults**; ensure the new site clearly states this where relevant (complex care, respite, disability).
5. **Navigation and content:** Align the new site with how families search: **Our Services** (Domiciliary, Respite, Complex as on live site), **About Us**, **Who You’ll Meet**, **Careers**, **Resources** (Care Guides, FAQs, Referral Information), **News & Updates**, and a clear **Contact / Free Care Consultation** path.
